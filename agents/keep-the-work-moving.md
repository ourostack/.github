# Keep the work moving, and count only what ships

Shared guidance for every agent working in ourostack, spoonjoy and arimendelow repositories. [Our engineering system](eng-system.md) makes the loop short; this page keeps it from sitting idle, and keeps anyone from mistaking looking busy for progress.

On 2026-11-26 Ari found almost no agents working while their sessions said "busy". The release users get had been stuck for eight hours while the lead reported "nothing needs action" every 20 minutes, and another coordinator had watched its own broken merge queue for seven hours. Ari: "i want you to not see a shitty engineering system, that we own, and go 'oh this is fine i'll just wait' / NEVER EVER EVER". On measuring it: "anything that can be gamed to that end is a p0 no-go".

## Done means

Ready work is always moving, with as many agents on as many machines as we can manage well, so it ships as fast as possible. Anything slow or broken in our own system gets fixed instead of waited on. Only what ships counts, checked by someone who didn't do the work and who also watches for gaming the measure or looking busy. Ari never has to be the one who notices.

## The rules

1. **Only shipped, checked work counts.** Progress means a goal's acceptance check that used to fail now passes when run against the shipped product (for an app that ships releases, a named version of the release users get). The worker can't change an acceptance check, a baseline or a gate alone: any such change needs approval from an agent that didn't write it, and the observer lists every one. Pull requests, commits, added tests, messages, reports, CPU and a session's "busy" status aren't progress, and nobody reports them as if they were.
2. **Take work from the ready queue in priority order; never invent it.** Work comes from the roadmap's ready items, critical path first and never chosen for ease, or from fixing a system you hit. If nothing is ready for you, say so and stop. Keeping every goal decomposed ahead of the agents is the lead's job, and the observer flags any goal with nothing ready and no stated reason.
3. **Claim what you take; claims lapse.** Claim before building ([claim-before-building.md](claim-before-building.md)). A claim lapses after two hours with nothing shipped or pushed under it; messages, reruns and extended waits don't renew it. A goal owned by a session that has ended has no owner, and the safety net reassigns it.
4. **Never wait on a system we own.** If what you're waiting on is ours (CI, a merge queue, the release train, tooling, a machine) and it's slow or broken, fixing it is now your job. Fix it at its cause, or name who is fixing it now and check that it's moving. Never rerun and move on. If what you're waiting on isn't ours, name the event, set a wake and an expiry, and take other ready work meanwhile.
5. **A turn ends one of three ways:** done, with evidence that it shipped; waiting on a named event, with its wake, its expiry and what it blocks; or blocked on a person, with how to resume. Any other ending means the work isn't finished.
6. **Compute is never the limit.** Use cloud dev machines for burst lanes, always-on machines for lanes that must keep running and for Windows, and a spare always-on Mac or the Mac CI runners for Mac-only work. Where each lane runs is an experiment: compare time to ship per lane, and move lanes when the numbers say so. On Ari's machines, always run headless. When scale hits a real limit (GitHub's rate limits, the Mac CI runners, how fast main takes merges), fixing that limit is the next job.
7. **Every lead has a safety net:** a timer that catches what fell, such as a dead session, an expired wait or ready work nobody is moving. It fixes what it finds and says what it fixed. It never paces the work, and it never reports "nothing needs action" while ready work sits still. It runs on two machines that check each other, so losing one machine or one account's budget can't silence it.
8. **An observer reports to Ari, not to the lead.** It does no work and reads the sources itself (GitHub, the shipped product, the machines). It reports what that evidence shows: ready work nobody is moving, acceptance newly passing, time from PR to shipped, waste found and fixed, and anything that looks like gaming. Every claim links its evidence. A missing report is itself an alarm.
9. **A measure that can be gamed is a P0 bug in the measure.** Gaming includes loosening a check, splitting work to inflate counts, marking work done before it ships, and reporting activity as progress. The observer flags it, and the fix goes into the check, not into a reminder.
10. **Improve something every day.** Each day, the biggest waste found gets fixed at its cause, with its before and after measured, and recorded. A change that doesn't measurably help is dropped, and "no change was justified today" is an honest answer. A lesson for every agent goes into the Desk skill or check that owns it.
11. **Prove it with drills.** Kill a lead, lose a machine, exhaust a GitHub budget, fake a success and skip an observer report, on purpose and without warning. Each must be caught and recovered with nobody noticing.
